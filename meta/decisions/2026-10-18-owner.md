## O-26 D-014: a replay day's cost stays flat; mission 20 starts with the speed units

Asked: the architect role's escalation after REVIEW-19 (2026-10-18, as its
stop notification gives it): "ruling: D-014, G1.3's time against O-3's
10-minute target and O-2's 20-minute cap, before mission 20". REVIEW-19
Notes 1: "The margin is now under 2 minutes on a lightly loaded machine, so
the next step that adds day-loop cost (S4.2's digest and issues) will
probably push G1.3 past the cap. D-014 is open for the owner. It needs a
ruling before mission 20, not after."

Decided: the goal is a flat cost per replay day within one process, not a
wall time. Within one process, a day's cost must not grow with the history
the state holds.

Reason, which constrains later work: G1.3 replays a year. A per-day cost
that grows with history makes the total grow with the square of the replay's
length, and every day-loop step adds to it.
- G1.3 took 542 s at mission 17 U5. In mission 19 it took 855 s at U1 (the
  table), 962 s at U4 (step 6) and 1049 s at the tip (step 7), and REVIEW-19
  measured 1087 s.
- Each day, `table.adjusted_deflated` calls `paper.adjusted` for every random
  control slot over all of an approach's paper days. The rules and the
  dashboard also read the whole history every day. That is the likely cause,
  read from the code and not measured.
- Decision 154 (c)'s figure covers only the scenario's first 30 days, where
  the history is short, so it cannot see this growth (REVIEW-11 already noted
  that "the per-day cost grows with history unprofiled").

1. **When.** Mission 20, after U0, begins with the speed units, before any
   unit of step S4.2. The role cuts them. If S4.2 no longer fits in seven
   units after them, the role splits the step as `meta/ARCHITECT.md` §5
   allows, and the speed units still come first.
2. **What they change.** Only how the day loop arrives at its figures:
   - the table (`table.build`);
   - step 6 (`alerts.rank_rows`, `alerts.detectors`, `alerts.step` and the
     rules);
   - step 7 (`dashboard.render`);
   - anything else the measurement of item 4 shows growing with history.

   A day reuses what earlier days of the same process computed, instead of
   reading and recomputing the whole history.
3. **What they do not change.**
   - What any day computes or writes. After a full replay of the G1.3
     scenario, the state directory at the unit's commit is byte-identical
     to the one at its parent, outside `.git/`. No new file appears in a
     state directory.
   - Caches. Any cache lives in the process. It is keyed by everything it
     reads, as decision 121 (a) keys the synthetic source, so that a
     changed record is computed again.
   - Tests and configuration: no gate test, no test's assertion, no span,
     count, threshold or frozen configuration value changes.
   - Dependencies: none added, and no worker process or thread.
   - Existing rules: O-18's register and O-25 apply as they do to every
     unit. A function a met gate reads keeps its result for today's inputs.
4. **Flat, measured.** The speed units add a measurement the project keeps:
   a command the role names. It replays the G1.3 scenario on the gate
   configuration, in foreground calls of at most 20 minutes each, and
   measures that one replay as follows.
   - A day's time runs from the end of the previous day to the end of this
     one. Everything before the first day ends is left out. So is the first
     day after any process start, since a new process begins with empty
     caches. The command reports where its process starts fell.
   - A day's subjects are the subjects that day's decision-journal rows
     name. A window's seconds per subject-day is the total time of its
     counted days divided by the total of their subjects.

   It prints four figures:
   - the seconds per subject-day over the scenario's first 30 calendar days
     (2019-06-03 to 2019-07-02);
   - the same over its last 30 (2020-05-12 to 2020-06-10);
   - their ratio, last over first;
   - the wall time of one fresh process that runs the scenario's last replay
     day on the state as it stood before that day. This is what a live run
     costs at that history, and it has no threshold.

   A ratio above 1.25 is a cost per subject-day that still grows with history, and
   it fails the unit. The unit is retried or marked blocked under the
   brief's rules, never passed. When the speed work is cut into several
   units, the ratio binds the last of them. Each earlier one gives the four
   figures and shows, from its profile at the first and the last 30 days,
   that the part it took no longer grows. The figures at the parent and at
   the commit go in the unit's body, with the profile.
5. **Equal to a full recompute.** Every figure computed incrementally
   equals what a full recompute from the records gives: the same value, not
   one within a tolerance, and the written files byte-identical.
   - A test proves it. It replays a scenario and, at several of its days,
     compares what was written incrementally with a full recompute from the
     records, every cache cleared: `table.json` and `table.md`, the alert
     state and payloads, and the dashboard page.
   - Its days include the first and the last, and a day with a lifecycle
     change where the replay has one.
   - The test runs in `./scripts/check`, within decision 140's bounds.
   - The speed units also run the same comparison over the whole G1.3
     scenario at their commit, at the first and last days and at a day after
     each kind of lifecycle change the scenario shows. They give the result
     in the body.
   - A figure that cannot be made equal to its full recompute is not made
     incremental. The unit files a finding for the owner (the next free
     `D-###`) instead.
6. **The live loop is not covered.** The live loop runs each day as a new
   process, which starts cold and reads the whole history, so its cost still
   grows linearly with history. That is accepted for a run once a day. Item
   4's fourth figure reports it, with no threshold.

## O-27 The 15-minute stop, and the times every later day-loop unit gives

Asked: as O-26.

Decided:
1. **Targets.** For every item of gate M1, O-3's 10-minute target is
   replaced by O-26's goal and by the stop in item 3. O-2 stands: each item
   is its own foreground call with a 20-minute timeout, and an item that
   does not finish within it is FAIL.
2. **What each day-loop unit gives.** From mission 20 on, every unit that
   changes the day loop, the speed units included, does two things at its
   commit:
   - it runs `./scripts/gate M1 G1.3` as one foreground call with a
     20-minute timeout, and gives the wall time and pytest's line;
   - it runs O-26's measurement and gives its four figures and where its
     process starts fell.

   These replace decision 154 (c)'s 30-day figure. A ratio above 1.25 fails
   the unit as O-26 item 4 says. The figures go in the unit's body and the
   mission's report, not in D-014 times lines.
3. **The stop.** An M1 item whose `./scripts/gate` call takes more than 15
   minutes of wall time stops the mission. This applies at a unit's commit
   and in the gate unit alike. The unit:
   - files a finding (the next free `D-###`) with the times and the
     measurement;
   - weakens no test, and changes nothing O-26 item 3 forbids in order to
     bring the time down.

   The builder then replies `VERDICT: question` naming the finding. When a
   review measures an M1 item above 15 minutes, the role reads it at its next
   consultation the same way: the next kit takes it before any other work,
   or the role escalates.
4. **D-014.** It is decided by O-26 and O-27. Mission 20's U0 appends, after
   the last line of `meta/findings/FINDINGS.md`: `D-014 status: decided
   meta/decisions/2026-10-18-owner.md (O-26, O-27)`.

REVIEW-19's should-fix items 1 to 3 and its notes are the role's, as usual.
