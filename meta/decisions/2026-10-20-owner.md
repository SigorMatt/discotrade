## O-28 D-024: each sampled day is recomputed over the span the contract bounds it to

Asked: the builder's question (job 0mvfhj89v-rw0f), verbatim: "D-024: decision
184's weekly recomputation took G1.3 to 934 s, over O-27's 15-minute stop, and
the median speed ratio to 2.473 against a limit of 1.25; should decision 184 /
DESIGN 9.4 give way, or O-26?". The driver escalated it (job 0mvg8q4pk-bwpq,
2026-10-20 08:26) as a design change. Finding D-024 gives the measurements:
- On the uncommitted U5 tree, a Monday cost about 2.4 to 3.1 s in June 2019
  and 14.7 to 18.8 s a year later.
- Mondays took 552 s of the span's 907 s.

Decided: neither gives way. What gives way is decision 54's span, for the
sample. DESIGN 9.4 asks for each sampled pair's rows to be recomputed from the
stored bars and the frozen sources and compared exactly. It does not ask for
the subject's whole history to be evaluated.

1. **The sample is unchanged.** It is drawn as (subject, day) pairs from each
   subject's whole journaled record before `D`, by the generator seeded with
   `D` (decision 54, `sample_pairs`):
   - on the first trading day of each ISO week (decision 184 (a));
   - and by `journal recompute` without `--all`, the same function.

   It is not drawn from recent days only, so old rows are still re-verified.
2. **Each pair's day is recomputed over the span the contract bounds it to,**
   not from the subject's first journaled day:
   - **an approach:** each target is a pure function of at most
     `history_cap_bars` stored bars ending at its bar (DESIGN 6.2, 7.1). The
     pair's day is evaluated from the stored bars with that context, as the
     day loop evaluates a day.
   - **a random control:** its machine starts flat at each draw's first bar
     (DESIGN 10.1). The pair's day is evaluated from the first bar of the
     draw in force that day, with the draws recorded in `pools.jsonl`.
   - **a buy-and-hold control:** the day itself.

   The carry into the day, meaning its first row's `prev_position` and
   `position`, is recomputed inside the same bound. It is never read from the
   stored journal. The position carries across a redraw, so on the first day
   of a monthly redraw the carry is the previous draw's last state. For that
   day the span starts at the first bar of the draw in force at the last bar
   before it. Where the span reaches the subject's first journaled day,
   DESIGN 9.2's first-row rule applies: flat.
3. **The comparison is unchanged.** It uses equal content hashes of the
   pair's rows on decision 49's keys. A mismatch is one `journal_mismatch`
   alert naming the pair (decision 184 (b)).
4. **`journal recompute --all` is unchanged.** It still evaluates each
   subject once, from its first journaled day (decision 54).
5. **The test,** added to decision 184 (c):
   - It replays a scenario long enough to hold the three cases below, and
     runs each through the same recompute function the sample uses:
     - a subject's first paper day;
     - a roll day;
     - the first day of a monthly redraw, for a random control.
   - The test chooses these pairs itself, so the draw cannot miss them. It
     picks the roll and redraw pairs so that the carry into each day is not
     flat.
   - Each bounded recompute equals the stored journal rows exactly (equal
     content hashes).
   - Seen to fail, each wrong change made alone and reverted:
     - the carry into the day taken as flat;
     - on a redraw's first day, the span started at the new draw.
6. **The gates stand.**
   - U5 gives G1.3 and decision 170's three measurements at its commit.
   - A median ratio above 1.25 fails it (O-26 item 4).
   - G1.3 above 15 minutes stops the mission (O-27 item 3).
   - Nothing that O-26 item 3 forbids changes.

Reason, which constrains later work: the bound comes from the contract, so a
bounded recompute compares exactly what a whole-history one compares, the
sampled pair's rows. Its cost per pair is one day's evaluation, the same in
the first week and the fiftieth, and the same in a replay and in a live run.
The test pins the three places where a day's rows depend on more than the
day: a subject's first row, a roll, and the carry across a redraw.
