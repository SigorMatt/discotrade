## O-33 HF-020: background moves on for this brief, and the speed measurement stays in the job

Asked: the builder's question (job 0mvkd6m8d-niwn), verbatim: "HF-020: this
session kills any foreground call at 20 minutes (`BASH_MAX_TIMEOUT_MS=1200000`)
and has background tasks turned off (`CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_BACKGROUND_TASKS=1`),
but U5's `./scripts/check` takes about 21–23 minutes, so it can never finish.
Should the role get a longer timeout (2700000, as HF-016 records), or should
the owner decide how a check over 20 minutes is run and counted?" The driver
escalated it (job 0mvkjazzj-55wc, 2026-10-23 08:33).

The hands chat's answer to the owner's report, 2026-10-23:
- the 20 minutes was the brief's own explicit timeout, not the job's limit;
- copying the hook (`9464acf`) turned hands' default for background tasks
  on, and no hand-over warned of it (hands H-089);
- a command started with `systemd-run --user --scope` leaves the job's
  scope. hands then neither tracks nor stops it (hands DESIGN §24), so a
  cancel, a usage-limit stop or the job's end leaves it running. O-32
  item 1's measurement does exactly that.

Decided:

1. **Background moves are back on for this brief.**
   - What the owner did, 2026-10-23 about 22:40: set
     `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_BACKGROUND_TASKS = "0"` in `[roles.builder.env]`
     and `[roles.aux.env]` of the project's hands config, then restarted the
     daemon with no job running. `BASH_DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_MS` and
     `BASH_MAX_TIMEOUT_MS` stay 2700000.
   - The effect: a call past its explicit timeout is moved to the background
     again and waited for as decision 176 says, so the running brief applies
     as written.
   - When the lines come out: once the switch brief (O-29, O-30) is in
     force, with the template's wait rule and a 30-minute explicit timeout.
     hands' default then turns background tasks off again.
2. **The speed measurement stays in the job.** It never leaves the role job:
   no `systemd-run` and no scope of its own.
   - **What it reads.** At each day boundary it sums, from `/proc`, the CPU
     time of its own process and of every live descendant: `utime + stime +
     cutime + cstime` from `/proc/<pid>/stat`, in clock ticks
     (`os.sysconf("SC_CLK_TCK")`).
   - **Why that is exact.** A child that has exited and been reaped is
     already in its parent's `cutime` and `cstime`; a live one is read
     itself. So the fork server and the sandbox children are counted, and
     the role's own Claude processes are not.
   - **The day's figure.** A day's CPU seconds are the change of that sum
     since the previous day boundary of the same call.
   - **What stays from O-32 item 1:** the windows, the exclusions, the ratio
     and its 1.25 limit, the fresh last day's CPU figure, and the refusal of
     a backend whose work runs outside the process tree, such as a container.
   - **No fallback.** If `/proc` cannot be read at the start or at a day
     boundary, the measurement stops with an error and never falls back to
     wall time. The builder then replies `VERDICT: question <the error>`.
   - O-32 items 2 to 9 stand: the load backstop, void runs, the binding
     median, the report, the unit's retry, the wall-time rules, and the rule
     standing from now on.
3. **Where it is built.** It is the second `scripts:` commit of U5's retry,
   made before U5's work is restored. `scripts/speed.py` and its tests change
   from the scope to the process tree. The load backstop and the void rule
   stay as `961dfe0` built them. The tests, with `/proc` replaced by a fake,
   prove four things:
   - a reaped child is counted once, in its parent's `cutime`, and a live
     child is read itself;
   - a descendant's CPU is counted on the day it was spent;
   - when `/proc` cannot be read, the run fails with an error and no
     fallback;
   - no command the measurement runs names `systemd-run`.

   One more test runs on the real subprocess backend, with no fake. A sandbox
   job burns a known amount of CPU, which it measures itself with
   `getrusage` and reports to the test. The test proves that this CPU is
   counted in the day it ran, within one clock tick for each process read,
   including when the day boundary is read after the fork server has reaped
   the child.

   The commit gives no G1.3 time and no ratio of its own.
4. **The tree is checked against the job's cgroup.**
   - **Which run.** The first valid run of U5 whose load samples average at
     most 2.0, O-32 item 8's figure for a quiet call. If that first valid run
     is busier, the next valid run that is quiet is used.
   - **What it reads.** In that run the measurement also reads, at each day
     boundary, `usage_usec` from the `cpu.stat` of the job's own cgroup
     (the path in `/proc/self/cgroup`, read only, never created). The unit's
     body gives, for each day, that cgroup's delta, the tree's figure and the
     gap between them.
   - **What the gap is.** It is the role's own Claude processes plus
     anything that escapes the tree. It must stay steady across the run: its
     mean over the last 30-day window may be at most 1.25 times its mean over
     the first, the factor O-26 allows the cost itself.
   - **A gap that grows more** means something escapes the tree. The measure
     is then not used until that is fixed: U5 counts none of its runs, and
     the builder replies `VERDICT: question <the two windows' mean gaps>`.
   - **If the job's cgroup is not the job's own scope,** named
     `hands-discotrade-<job>.scope`, no comparison can be made. The builder
     replies `VERDICT: question <the cgroup it found>`.

Reason, which constrains later work: a measurement must stay inside what
hands can stop. The process tree counts the replay and nothing else, so the
1.25 limit means what O-26 item 4 meant.
